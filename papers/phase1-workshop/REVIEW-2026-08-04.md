**What this file is.** The successor `REVIEW-2026-08-01.md`'s stamp now names. It
reviews the 2026-08-04 edit in the hostile register the 07-30 pass established:
the question is not whether the numbers are right — `CITECHECK-2026-08-04.md`

## The four objections a referee will actually raise

**1. "A negative result with three explanations is not a result, it is a
failure to design the experiment."** This is the strongest objection and the
paper should not pretend otherwise. The honest answer is in the ordering: §11.3b
does not present three explanations as equally live, it gives each one a
measurement that would kill it, prices them at \$0, \$9 and \$120, and states
that the \$120 one is running while the \$0 one has never been done. A referee
who reads that as an admission of poor sequencing is reading it correctly; the
paper's position is that recording the sequencing error is better than
publishing the zero as a capability finding, which is what the previous draft
did. **The register is right and the paper should expect to be marked down for
the content.**

**2. "Two of your four games do give a capability result, so why is the headline
negative?"** Because `ar25` and `tn36` are the two games where six *haiku* runs
reached the reference cost. No `opus-5` or `sonnet-5` run ever did, on any game,
so the capability evidence is entirely on the weakest tier — and the tier the
paper's comparisons are about has zero adequate-budget runs. §7.10a states both
halves. A referee could reasonably ask for the headline to be "the strongest
model has never been tested at adequate budget", which is arguably the sharper
sentence; it is not adopted because it is a claim about *why* the corpus is empty
and §11.3b's whole point is that the paper cannot yet say why.

**3. "The prediction-then-confirmation result is one point on one game."** Yes,
and §11.3a says so in the paragraph that reports it: n = 24 on `g50t`, n = 3 on
`sk48`, the pooled figure lower than the prediction for that reason, and the
sk48 leg reported as a count rather than a rate. What survives is narrow — an
offline harness sized one change correctly before it was paid for. The temptation
was to report 83 % → 83 % as agreement without saying that the round-level number
is 77.8 %; the paragraph reports both, and names the pooling as the reason they
differ. **Checked site by site**, because reporting only the leg that matched is
precisely the failure this section documents elsewhere.

**4. "You corrected four numbers and all four had been wrong in your favour.
Why should the fifth be right?"** It should not be assumed to be. The answer the
edit gives is mechanical rather than rhetorical: every figure in the new text is
recomputed by a script that prints AGREES/DIFFERS per row against the source
arm's own published number, and the script is committed beside the prose
(`papers/phase1-workshop/runs/20260804T1500Z-P19b-the-zero-and-its-three-explanations/census.py`).
That does not make the paper trustworthy; it makes it checkable, which is the
only property it claims in §11.5.

## Where this delta is weaker than it reads

* **The "several hundred actions" extrapolation in §11.3b's second
  explanation is an extrapolation.** It multiplies the best observed
  probe-to-advance ratio by seventeen. Nothing in the corpus tests whether that
  ratio holds at scale, and the sentence says "implies" rather than "would
  cost" for that reason. A referee is entitled to strike it; the surrounding
  claim — largest non-probe count is 11 against a reference of 78 — stands
  without it.
* **The 16-leg count depends on a definition.** Fifteen legs carry
  `env_proxy.upstream`; the sixteenth is a first-contact run whose record was
  rebuilt from its ledger after the process was killed. Both numbers are printed
  and the census prints the rule it used. A referee who prefers 15 loses nothing:
  no claim turns on the sixteenth.
* **The third explanation is argued from the absence of a file, not from a
  demonstrated failure.** Twenty-two zero-byte level logs prove the write never
  happened; they do not prove the write *would* fail if a level were completed.
  §11.3b says "might not have been recorded", not "would not have been", and the
  \$0 experiment it names is exactly the one that would settle it. This is the
  weakest of the three arguments and it is the one the section leans on hardest,
  which is worth saying out loud.
* **Two paragraphs of the previous draft are quoted in order to be withdrawn.**
  That is the house style here, and it makes §11.3a longer than a workshop
  budget wants. E1 in `OPEN_ITEMS.md` — length against ~4,000 words — gets worse
  by roughly 900 words with this edit and is not addressed.
