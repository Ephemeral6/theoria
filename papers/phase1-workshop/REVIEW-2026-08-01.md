**What this file is.** The successor `REVIEW-2026-07-31.md`'s stamp names. It
reviews only the 2026-08-01 correction, in the same adversarial register the
predecessor used: each item is a way the *correction itself* could mislead, and
what the text does about it. The paper moved 232 lines under this delta; nothing
outside the edited sites changed, so the standing reviews' findings are neither
re-litigated nor silently re-endorsed.

## Ways the corrected text could still mislead, examined

1. **"The probe mechanism does not work" read as "probing is useless."** The
   measurement is about one frontier construction on four legs of two
   development-pile games. It says the ablation family cannot contain a mechanism
   the manual lacks — a structural fact, not a sampling result — and it says the
   observed answers fell outside it 47 times in 52. It does **not** say that a
   frontier-splitting probe is a bad idea, and §11.3a's decomposition (35 drift,
   12 expressivity, 0 action choice) is precisely the sentence that stops the
   over-read: the arm was choosing good actions and asking about the wrong state.
   *Adequate, and the risk is real enough that §2.3 repeats the scope.*

2. **The repair reads as a result.** This is the most likely misreading and it is
   guarded four times: the generated frontier is stated as default-off, as
   byte-identical on the ablation path, as replayed against recorded states rather
   than forecast, and as never once run live. A reviewer looking for the sentence
   "43 of 52" without its qualifier will not find it — every occurrence carries
   the counterfactual caveat in the same block. *Adequate.* The residual risk is
   the abstract, where "a repair exists, is default-off, and has offline replay
   evidence only" is compressed; the compression is deliberate and the number is
   not quoted there at all.

3. **The 48-of-48 refusal reads as an engine defect.** It is not presented as
   one, and it should not be: the engine returned a refusal *with a written
   reason* every time, which is the behaviour a candidate-emitting engine is
   supposed to have when it cannot mine. The finding is about the **supply
   line** — the design assigns the frontier's construction to a component that has
   never delivered one on a game — not about the component's correctness. §2.2 and
   §11.3a both say "refused with a written reason". *Adequate.* A residual: the
   paper does not adjudicate whether the refusals were right, because that would
   require reading the mechanics of the games and is another territory's call.

4. **Citing `Theoria.md` against the implementation could look like scoring a
   point off a design document.** The correction's force depends on the design
   document asking for the opposite of what was built, so the text quotes the two
   phrases that carry it (全体一致假设的前沿, 戳探的原料) rather than paraphrasing
   them. A reader who disagrees can open one line. *Adequate.*

5. **"Zero levels completed" could be read as this paper's headline failing.**
   The paper never claimed a completed level, and §1.6 already says every pipeline
   result is offline on self-built worlds. The sentence is in §11.3a to close a
   different hole: a reader who has just read about live legs, live spend and a
   live round protocol would otherwise be entitled to assume something was won.
   *Adequate, and the placement is the point.*

6. **Ten legs versus six.** §11.3a says ten and names the six the battery reads
   as a subset; the battery's own artefact says `n_runs: 6` because four legs
   landed after it was written. Quoting only one of the two numbers would have
   made the other look like an error. *Adequate.*

7. **§11.4a's three items are not the same kind of thing, and the section could
   flatten them.** One is a bug that was fixed (the lost turn), one is a check
   that was wrong and was loosened (the name matcher), and one is a headline
   number that keeps its value and loses its meaning (the battery demotion). The
   subsection's opening sentence says all three are results about measurement and
   none is a result about world models, and each paragraph names its own kind.
   *Adequate, though the third is the one a hostile reader will press on, because
   it weakens a claim §1.5 lists as this paper's strongest contribution.*

8. **The demotion is stated in §11.4a and §11.5 but §1.5 item 1 and §7 keep the
   old framing.** This is a real inconsistency of emphasis and it is **left
   standing deliberately**: §1.2's five limits already qualify the number, §11.5
   now carries the demotion explicitly, and rewriting §7 would be a re-audit of a
   chapter this delta did not open. *Recorded as a defect of arrangement, not
   repaired.* A reader who reads only §1.5 gets a number that is true and an
   interpretation that §11.4a weakens.

9. **The delta cites another territory's manifest for the two most persuasive
   numbers.** The 35/12/0 decomposition and the 43-of-52 replay cannot be
   reproduced from this repository, because their inputs are gitignored. The
   accompanying `CITECHECK-2026-08-01.md` grades every claim **recomputed /
   read / derived** for this reason, and §11.3a states the gitignored input in
   prose. *Adequate as disclosure; it does not make the numbers checkable.*

10. **The correction is written by the same project that made the error.** No
    independent adversary reviewed this delta. That is the same weakness P18's
    ruling recorded about its own round, and it is stated rather than left for a
    reader to notice.

## What this review did not cover

The unchanged text; the figures; the bibliography; anything in §3–§10. It made no
ARC call, no model call and no network request, and touched no sealed-pile game.
