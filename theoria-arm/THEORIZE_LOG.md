# THEORIZE_LOG — the Theoria arm's first online contact

Every proposal the engines made, and what was done with it. The per-round
machine record is `runs/<slug>/TIMELINE.md`, generated by
`armtools/timeline.py` from the book snapshots, the adjudication logs and the
surprise register — this file is the reading of it.

Verdicts, as in A0 and A2: **accept** (written into `theory.dsl`) · **reject** ·
**entailed** (true, but already implied by something else) · **probe-pending**
(constraint 7: a theorem that has not been probed is not final). `E-` entries
are things the world said that the DSL cannot.

---

## What is different about this one

The four previous cold starts (`a0-spike`, `cold-start-a0`, A1,
`cold-start-a2`) all adjudicated a world that had been **built to be
adjudicated**: 9×9 or 12×12 boards, one mover, a few hundred transitions, and a
ground truth in the next directory. This is the first time the loop has read a
world nobody designed for it, through a 64×64 frame, on an evidence budget of a
few dozen transitions, with no ground truth anywhere.

Three of the framework's own mechanisms met their first real test as a result,
and all three bent in the same direction: **the engines are calibrated for
evidence this run does not have.** That is the through-line of everything
below, and it is a finding about the framework rather than about this game.

---

## The seal

There is no ground truth to seal against — ARC ships none, and
`baseline-arms/schema_traces/` (which covers this game, legally, on the
development pile) was deliberately not read, because feeding another arm's
trajectory to this one would give Theoria evidence bare-CC never had. Every
frame adjudicated below, this arm paid for.

What replaces the seal is that the desk is sealed *from the repository*:
`ModelDesk` starts `claude -p` in an empty directory outside the repo, so the
desk has never seen `Theoria.md`, the pile cut, the cold starts' worked
answers, or its own source. What it knows about the framework is what
`inner/theorize.py`'s preamble tells it, and that preamble is in the run
archive.

The hole that remains, named as A0 named its own: **the same instance wrote the
arm and reads its output.** The adjudications below are written to be checkable
against `candidates.jsonl` and the frame renderings alone.

## O — objects

### O-01 · The concept account went negative on first contact, and the framework's two admission criteria disagreed again — but for a new reason

`mdl_segmenter` proposed six object hypotheses off six states. Their MDL
account, from the engine's own cost model:

| | script bits | pixel baseline | gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| all six tracks | 7018 | 1976 | **−5042** |

Compression ratio **3.55** — the segmentation costs three and a half times what
encoding the changed pixels raw would. `Theoria.md` §1.8 says a concept's
ticket of admission is that it makes the manual shorter, and constraint 5
forbids an entry with no gain. By that rule **every object here should be
rejected**, and the manual would then have nothing to say at all.

A0 and A2 both hit a version of this: their Button and Door failed the
compression test (−17/−13 and −5/−1) and were admitted anyway, because
constraint 2's full-frame responsibility left no alternative — a pixel that
changes cannot be board, so if it is not an object it is unexplained forever.
That conflict is reproduced here, and this run adds something to it rather than
restating it:

**In A0 the compression account was merely too small. Here it is inverted, and
the reason is the evidence budget, not the world.** MDL amortises a track's
declaration cost over the frames it appears in. Six states cannot amortise
anything: the declaration is paid once and the savings accrue once. A0 read its
+2967 off 275 transitions. The same world observed for 275 transitions would
very likely show a positive account, and nothing about the *world* changed
between the two readings — only how long anyone had watched.

So the finding is sharper than "the criteria disagree". It is: **the
compression criterion is not scale-free, and at first contact it points the
wrong way.** A framework that admitted concepts strictly by MDL would be unable
to form its first concept on any world, which cannot be the intent of §1.8.
Carried to the report as the first framework finding of the online run.

### O-02 · One "object" is the level, and the segmenter said so loudly

`obj3` is a 50×38 track of 1006 cells with `color: null` — the level's
structure, merged into a single component. This is the documented degradation
of choosing the modal colour as background (`world/frames.background()`): when
the modal colour is a floor the segmentation is clean, and when it is not the
result is one enormous track. It is exactly the failure that docstring
predicted, and it failed *visibly*, which is what was wanted — a silent version
of this would have produced a plausible-looking object nobody could check.

### O-03 · Colour is the only handle, and two objects sharing one colour cannot be told apart

The arm locates a declared object by its colour in the first frame
(`# arc-colour: <n>`), so a manual that declares two objects of the same colour
gets two instances at the same cell. The grammar card says so and tells the
desk to declare one object and carry the belief that there are two as a
`theorem`. This is a real expressivity limit of *this arm*, not of the DSL, and
it is logged as `E-` rather than hidden.

## R — rules

The per-round tables are in `runs/<slug>/TIMELINE.md`. Four adjudications are
worth reading out here because of what they show about the *discipline*, not
about `g50t`.

### R-01 · The desk wrote two ground rules where it wanted one schema, and said so

`step_down_from_home` and `key5_sends_ring_home` name the only two maze cells
ever witnessed. The desk's own note on why:

> maze cell (r,c) spans rows 8+6r..12+6r and cols 14+6c..18+6c … The ring moved
> 6 pixels at t2 and 6 pixels back at t5. `moved(o,dir)` steps one cell, so I
> could not write a general step rule and wrote two `jumped()` rules naming the
> only two positions ever witnessed. **This manual therefore predicts NOTHING
> about any maze cell other than (0,0) and (1,0).**

That last sentence is the whole point of constraint 5 working. The desk had the
generalisation — it had *derived the grid pitch* — and it declined to write it,
because the event vocabulary cannot express a six-pixel step and inventing one
would have made the manual look stronger than its evidence. The pitch is
carried as `theorem grid_pitch_six [probe: pending]` instead.

Compare A0's R-05, which met the same shape from the other side: there the
generalisation was expressible and unwitnessed, and was rejected for lack of
evidence. Here it is witnessed and inexpressible. **Two different walls, same
answer: it goes in `laws:` as a theorem, not in `rules:` as a rule.**

### R-02 · A direction mapping held as a theorem because every observation is also consistent with nothing

> I believe key(1)=up, key(2)=down, key(3)=left, key(4)=right. Evidence: key(2)
> moved the ring from cell (0,0) to (1,0) (t2); key(1) fired from the top row
> and key(3) from the leftmost column and both changed nothing (t1,t3); key(4)
> fired from (1,0) whose right neighbour … is colour 0, i.e. not floor, and the
> ring did not move (t4). **Every one of these is also consistent with the key
> being unbound, which is why this is a theorem and not a rule.**

Three of the four keys have exactly the evidence profile of a key that does
nothing. The desk noticed and refused to bank it. This is the single cleanest
instance in the run of the loop's central discipline: *the evidence is
compatible with two readings, so nothing enters the manual until an experiment
separates them* — and it is precisely the case `probe` exists to settle, since
firing key(1) from a cell that is *not* on the top row is a one-action
experiment.

### R-03 · The desk found this arm's own defect from the inside

> colour 9 paints four different things: the player ring, HUD token A … the
> selection underline, the bracket around maze cell (7,5), and the row-63 bar.
> **One colour binds one object**, so Player takes 9 and the 11 dynamic
> colour-9 HUD pixels of frames 0-4 plus the 6 underline pixels have no object
> and will be reported unexplained. I believe there are at least three distinct
> colour-9 entities; **the arm cannot tell them apart, so I said so here
> instead of pretending.**

"One colour binds one object" is `E-03` — a limitation of *this arm's* level
generator, not of the DSL and not of the world. The desk was told about it in
the grammar card, worked out what it would cost in unexplained pixels, and
predicted the exact responsibility failure certify would report before certify
ran. A manual that predicts its own certify failure is a better artefact than
one that passes quietly.

### R-04 · An event the vocabulary does not have

> row 63 is a bar of colour 9 that fills with colour 1 from the right edge …
> **I cannot express one further pixel turning 1 — there is no event for
> growing an object** — so the manual leaves (63,62) and (63,63) unexplained in
> the frames where they are still 9.

A counter that fills one pixel at a time has no event form: `recolored(o, c)`
recolours a whole object, and there is no `grew`. Logged as `E-05`.

### R-00 · `cegis_miner` proposed nothing, and the refusal is the finding

Zero `rule_hypothesis` rows. `transitions_from_segmentation` raises unless
every transition narrates **exactly one `move` event or none**, and no track in
this world satisfies that. The precondition is a substantive claim — *this
world has one mover and nothing else changes when it moves* — and this world is
not like that: single commands here change 49 and 71 cells at once and return
up to nine frames.

The rule-mining engine, the one that produces the hypothesis frontier that
probe design consumes, therefore contributed **nothing** on first contact.
Every rule in the manual below was written by the desk from the frame diffs
directly. That is the division of labour bending: `Theoria.md` 1.10(b) puts
rule mining in the engine column precisely so the LLM does not have to guess at
it, and on this world the engine could not take the job.

Recorded, not worked around (D-P8-006). Reshaping the input until the miner
answered would have made it answer a question it was not asked.

## L — laws

### L-01 · Seventy "global laws" from six states, and why none of them is a law

`zero_space` returned 362 GF(2) laws, 70 of them global, of the form
`(c9@0 + c9@3) mod 2 = 1` — "these two cells always disagree in colour 9".

They are all true over the observed states and **none of them is a
conservation law**. The arithmetic says why: the null space of the difference
matrix has dimension `features − rank`, the rank is bounded by the number of
transitions, and with a handful of transitions against a few hundred features
nearly every vector is in the null space. A0 read its two laws — `cart_unique`
and the `door_latch` that was the single most valuable line in its whole
candidate stream — off **275** transitions.

The engine is behaving correctly and the evidence is not. Rather than filter
the laws (which would hide the problem) or pass them on (which would invite the
desk to write a correlation into `laws:` as though it were proven), the arm now
computes the adequacy and hands the verdict to the desk with the laws:
`evidence_adequacy.verdict` reads `THIN` and names the rank, the feature count
and the null-space dimension. A law offered under that verdict is a
*correlation awaiting a transition that could break it*, and the desk is told
to say so in `status:`.

**This is the same finding as O-01 in a different engine**: the precise methods
are calibrated for a volume of evidence that first contact does not have, and
the honest response is to say which regime you are in rather than to report the
output at face value.

### L-02 · The proof layer was never available

Not "failed" — *unavailable*. Lean's enumerative development decides every
state in the kernel and this level's state space is astronomically larger than
the 200,000 ceiling; the pagoda development needs a LINE world and an
`lp_potential` certificate, and this is a grid world with no state graph at
all. `certify.expensive` reports `available: false` with the estimate that
caused it, and the run's `green` flag never goes true.

Both of the framework's two Lean routes are therefore shut on a real ARC level.
That is the most consequential limit this run met: `Theoria.md`'s two-layer
truth regime (Lean guarantees truth *relative to the manual*; probes test the
manual against the world) has only one layer here.

## E — expressivity ledger

Things the world said that the DSL, the compiler, or this arm could not.

| # | wanted | what happened | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| E-01 | two events from one rule (`then moved(A, down) and moved(B, up)`) | `ParseError: Invalid function call`. Observed in the offline dry run: the desk inferred the syntax from `cascade single_frame` and the guard grammar's `and`, and it does not exist. | the desk must split one observed simultaneity into two rules with identical guards, which `conflict exclusive` then has to be told is not an ambiguity |
| E-02 | a landmark's coordinates | the DSL has nowhere to write them (correctly — they are level data), but the arm supplied no `landmarks` either, so every manual declaring one failed to compile | one aborted run and $1.31; fixed by `# arc-cell: (r, c)` on the declaration line |
| E-03 | a literal colour in the word table | `object Cart { color: Int }` names the field, never the value, so the arm cannot find the object in the frame | `# arc-colour: <n>` on the declaration line, read by the arm, invisible to the compiler |
| E-04 | a click action with coordinates | the guard language has no way to say "act on cell (r, c)" | not needed on `g50t` (`available_actions` is `[1..5]`), but it shuts the click family out of this arm entirely; the card tells the desk to raise it as a `theorem`, never to invent syntax |
| E-05 | one pixel of an object turning a new colour | `recolored(o, c)` recolours a whole object and there is no `grew(o)`; a counter that fills a pixel at a time cannot be written | the manual leaves the row-63 tally bar unexplained in the frames where it has partly filled, and says so |
| E-06 | a rule schema over cells at a fixed pitch (`moved` by six) | `moved(o, dir)` is one cell and `jumped(o, landmark)` needs a named destination, so a regular grid of cells needs one landmark and one rule *per cell* | the manual covers the two cells witnessed and explicitly predicts nothing about the other 46; the pitch is carried as a theorem |
| E-07 | prose in an `invariant` | `invariant` bodies must contain a comparison operator; only `theorem` takes a sentence. The desk conflated them and the parse failed with "No comparison op in invariant" | one repair round, $1.33. The card now states the rule in the imperative and a test pins both halves — this one was a prompt bug, not a language limit |

E-02 and E-03 are the same shape and worth naming as one: **the domain/problem
split is real and the DSL enforces it, but the arm has to carry the problem
data across the boundary somehow, and comments are where it ended up.** A
manual read by a human is unaffected; a manual read by the compiler needs a
level file it has no way to request.

## The result: the loop cycles rather than converges, and the count says so

Unexplained pixels at frame 0, across the four certify rounds of the first
contact:

```
69 → 68 → 69 → 69
```

Four rewrites, five desk calls, $6.32, and the responsibility failure ends
where it began. This is the run's headline finding and it should not be
softened into "early days".

**Why it cycles is legible, and that is the interesting part.** The desk is not
failing to see the problem — it diagnosed it in round one, in
`theorem colour_nine_collision`, before certify had reported anything:

> One colour binds one object, so Player takes 9 and the 11 dynamic colour-9
> HUD pixels of frames 0-4 plus the 6 underline pixels have no object and will
> be reported unexplained. I believe there are at least three distinct colour-9
> entities; the arm cannot tell them apart, so I said so here instead of
> pretending.

The defect is `E-03` — this arm locates an object by its colour, so two objects
sharing a colour are one object — and **no rewriting of `theory.dsl` can fix
it**, because the manual is not where the defect lives. Each round the desk
re-derives the same diagnosis, rewords the manual around it, and the count
returns.

`Theoria.md` 1.10(d) says the loop is driven by surprise. It is silent on what
happens when the same surprise fires against a defect the manual has no way to
address, and this run is that case: **the loop will spend one model call per
cycle, forever, until something outside the manual changes.** Two things follow
and neither is a criticism of the framework:

1. **A surprise that recurs unchanged is different in kind from a surprise that
   recurs smaller.** The arm now has the number that distinguishes them —
   `cells_unexplained` — and it is the obvious input to a rule that stops
   calling the desk about a surprise it cannot answer. The evidence gate
   (`MIN_NEW_FRAMES_BETWEEN_THEORIZE`) is a blunter version of this: it at least
   makes each cycle buy more evidence.
2. **The expressivity ledger is not a footnote, it is the work queue.** `E-03`
   is the single thing standing between this manual and a green responsibility
   check, and it is an arm defect, not a DSL limit — the DSL is perfectly
   capable of declaring three objects; this arm cannot *find* them, because it
   looks them up by colour.

The measurement itself is the win. Constraint 2's full-frame responsibility
pass produced a real, moving quantity on a real 64×64 frame, and that quantity
was able to say *you are going in circles*. A check that could only report pass
or fail could not have said it, and four more rounds would have been spent
before anyone noticed.
